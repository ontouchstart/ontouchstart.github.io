What is Baader–Meinhof phenomenon?

Gemma 4 31B:
The Baader–Meinhof phenomenon, also known as frequency illusion, is a cognitive bias where something you recently learned or noticed for the first time suddenly seems to appear everywhere. It is not that the thing has actually become more common, but rather that your brain is now subconsciously scanning for it.

This happens due to two simultaneous psychological processes. The first is selective attention: when you encounter a new piece of information, your brain flags it as important, making you more likely to notice it in your environment. The second is confirmation bias: each new sighting reinforces your belief that the item has suddenly surged in popularity or frequency, leading you to ignore all the times you didn't see it in the past.

Despite these critiques, Baudelaire’s obsession with the "modern" and the ephemeral—the very things photography is best at capturing—created a fascinating irony. He wanted to capture the "fugitive" beauty of the city, yet he rejected the one tool capable of freezing that moment in time.
